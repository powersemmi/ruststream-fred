    Benchmarks

    What ruststream-fred costs on every message: throughput against the raw fred client, and instructions and allocations of its code.

    Against a raw client​

    The first chart runs each scenario three ways on a real Redis server. Raw client is a loop written by hand on fred. Broker crate is the same loop written on this crate: the broker, the subscription, the stream of deliveries and the ack, with no runtime. RustStream is the service a user writes, a #[subscriber] handler in an app. The first gap is what this crate costs over the client it wraps, and the second is what the runtime adds on top.

    Each loop is a bar from its best round to its slowest, with a dot at the best round and a notch at the median. The zero of the axis is the raw client's best round, and a dot right of zero is slower. A hollow dot is a loop whose difference from the raw client is smaller than the spread of the rounds. The table under the chart prints the best round with the median in parentheses.

    Three forms are measured, one per transport: a stream read through a consumer group that acknowledges every entry, a reliable list, and a Pub/Sub channel. Each runs against a standalone server, a cluster and a primary behind Sentinel. On the cluster the Pub/Sub row is sharded, SSUBSCRIBE and SPUBLISH, the form a cluster is used with.

    Cost of the code​

    The second chart counts what one message costs on the service's thread: instructions under callgrind and allocations under DHAT, in the steady state. Each scenario is a service on RedisBroker against the standalone server of the stand. The service runs on a single-threaded runtime, and fred drives its connections on the same thread, so the count holds the runtime, this crate, and fred writing the commands and parsing the replies. The server is another process and is not counted. Cold start is what connecting the pool, creating the group, opening the subscription and taking the first delivery cost once.

    A stream scenario reads a consumer group with XREADGROUP, settles with XACK and replies with XADD. A reliable list claims each entry with BLMOVE and settles it with LREM. A Pub/Sub scenario reads a channel or a pattern and settles nothing. A .pipeline() scenario sends the settles of a read together and its handler queues one INCR per delivery, and .pipeline().atomic() wraps each delivery's segment in MULTI and EXEC, as Window draws.
